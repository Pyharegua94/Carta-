Dear Members of the Columbia School of Economics Department,

I am writing to express my serious concern regarding the recent events unfolding in Paraguay involving Ueno Bank and the broader political and financial controversy surrounding President Santiago Peña, whose previous business ties to the financial group associated with Ueno Bank have become a matter of public scrutiny.

The crisis has had profound implications for Paraguay, affecting millions of citizens and raising serious questions about financial oversight, conflicts of interest, institutional accountability, and the protection of public resources.

In light of these circumstances, I believe it is reasonable for Columbia University to examine the circumstances surrounding Costanza Peña’s enrollment and any relevant conduct that warrants institutional scrutiny.

Columbia University is an internationally respected academic institution whose reputation is built upon principles of integrity, accountability, ethical leadership, and social responsibility. These principles should be upheld consistently, particularly when individuals connected to public power become subjects of significant public concern.

I therefore respectfully request that the University review this matter, assess any relevant evidence of direct involvement or conduct that may violate its institutional standards, and consider appropriate disciplinary measures, up to and including termination of enrollment, should the findings justify such action.

This request is not intended to establish guilt by family association. Rather, it is a call for transparency, institutional accountability, and the consistent application of the standards Columbia University expects of its students.

The people of Paraguay deserve accountability, transparency, and justice. Institutions of international standing should take these concerns seriously and demonstrate that their values are more than statements on paper.

I respectfully urge Columbia University to give this matter the attention it deserves.

Sincerely,

A Concerned Paraguayan Citizen
